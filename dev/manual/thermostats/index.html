Temperature control

Use a thermostat to sample at a target temperature, or a Langevin method to model friction and stochastic forcing. Temperature inputs are expressed as kT in energy units.

Stochastic velocity rescaling

all = Group(:all, AllSelection())
integrator = Integrator(
    dt=0.002,
    forces=[LennardJones(epsilon=1.0, sigma=1.0, cutoff=2.5)],
    methods=[ConstantVolume(all; thermostat=CSVR(kT=1.0, tau=0.1))],
)

tau sets the thermostat response time. The single-temperature example combines CSVR with thermodynamic measurements and collision counting.

Nosé–Hoover chains

To use a deterministic chain thermostat, replace the method above with:

ConstantVolume(all; thermostat=NoseHooverChain(
    kT=1.0, tau=0.1, chain_length=5, substeps=4,
))

Monitor equilibration and temperature fluctuations before collecting production averages. A target temperature does not make every instantaneous measurement equal to that target.

Langevin baths

For Langevin dynamics, specify friction and temperature on the method itself:

Langevin(all; gamma=10.0, kT=1.0)

Select a compatible integration scheme. Different particle groups can have different bath parameters; see the two-temperature mixture.

Equilibrate, then run NVE

After equilibration, replace the integrator's methods with ConstantVolume(all) and call prepare!(sim). The first simulation shows the complete pattern.

The workflow reference documents CSVR, NoseHooverChain and Langevin.
