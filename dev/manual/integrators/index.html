Dynamics

Choose a method for the physics and a scheme for the numerical integration. The Integrator combines them with your forces and timestep.

DynamicsMethodScheme
Microcanonical MD (NVE)ConstantVolume(all)VelocityVerlet()
Thermostatted MD (NVT)ConstantVolume(all; thermostat=CSVR(kT=1.0, tau=0.1))VelocityVerlet()
Langevin dynamicsLangevin(all; gamma=1.0, kT=1.0)VelocityVerlet(), BAOAB(), BAOA() or GSM()
Overdamped Brownian dynamicsBrownian(all; gamma=1.0, kT=1.0)EulerHeun() or EulerMaruyama()

Here all = Group(:all, AllSelection()); kT is an energy in your chosen units.

Molecular dynamics

For NVE, provide initial velocities and leave the system without a thermostat:

integrator = Integrator(
    dt=0.002,
    scheme=VelocityVerlet(),
    forces=[LennardJones(epsilon=1.0, sigma=1.0, cutoff=2.5)],
    methods=[ConstantVolume(all)],
)

The NVE example includes velocity initialization and energy output. For NVT, add a thermostat. For NPT, see pressure control.

Brownian dynamics

Use an overdamped method when inertia is not part of your model:

integrator = Integrator(
    dt=0.00025,
    scheme=EulerMaruyama(),
    forces=[WCA(epsilon=10.0, sigma=1.0)],
    methods=[Brownian(all; gamma=10.0, kT=1.0)],
)

Friction must be positive on stochastic paths that divide by it. A timestep that works for an inertial system need not work for an overdamped one; check displacement and the observables relevant to your model.

Active forcing

ActiveOrnsteinUhlenbeck adds a correlated stochastic process. Thermal noise is specified separately through Brownian or Langevin, so both methods can act on the same group:

methods = [
    Brownian(all; gamma=10.0, kT=1.0),
    ActiveOrnsteinUhlenbeck(all; gamma=10.0, tau=1.0, noise_scale=1.0),
]

noise_scale is the stationary standard deviation of the OU impulse; it is not a bath temperature. For multiple correlation times, supply an OUSpectrum. The active-particle examples include free-particle displacement checks.

Choose and change a timestep

Timesteps must be finite and positive. Start conservatively, then compare runs at smaller timesteps using energy conservation, structural observables or transport coefficients appropriate to your system.

A stage can override the configured timestep, for example during warmup:

run!(sim, Stage(:warmup, steps=1_000; dt=0.0001))
run!(sim, Stage(:production, steps=10_000))

If you replace sim.integrator, call prepare!(sim) before continuing. Changing stochastic settings can rebuild stochastic workspaces; a continued run is not a promise of bitwise replay of an uninterrupted noise history.
