Electrostatics

ParticleDynamics computes long-range Coulomb interactions natively on the GPU with three particle-mesh solvers that share one mesh engine:

DescriptorMethodSplitAssignmentDifferentiationTransforms per force call
PMEsmooth particle-mesh EwaldGaussiancardinal B-splineanalytic1 forward, 1 inverse
PPPM(differentiation=:ad)P3M, optimal influence functionGaussianB-splineanalytic1 forward, 1 inverse
PPPM(differentiation=:ik)P3M, optimal influence functionGaussianB-splinereciprocal-space field1 forward, 3 inverse
ESPEwald summation with prolatesprolate (PSWF)prolate windowanalytic1 forward, 1 inverse

Every step of a force evaluation runs on the device: real-space pairs, exception corrections, charge spreading, cuFFT R2C/C2R transforms with preallocated plans, the spectral solve (with Float64 energy and virial reductions), and the force gather. Host code validates the input, generates coefficients and plans transforms at setup only. A warmed force call performs no device allocations.

Supported scope: 3D orthorhombic periodic boxes, conducting (tin-foil) boundary conditions, uniform relative permittivity, fixed charges, a single GPU, Float32 or Float64. 2D, slab, triclinic and open-boundary electrostatics are rejected at construction.

Quick example

using ParticleDynamics

n, a = 16, 2.82                                  # Å
L = n * a
pos = [(a * (i - 0.5) - L / 2, a * (j - 0.5) - L / 2, a * (k - 0.5) - L / 2)
       for i in 1:n for j in 1:n for k in 1:n]
typeids = Int32[isodd(i + j + k) ? 1 : 2 for i in 1:n for j in 1:n for k in 1:n]
system = ParticleSystem(pos; box=(L, L, L), types=[:Na, :Cl], typeids=typeids,
                        masses=Dict(:Na => 22.99, :Cl => 35.45),
                        charges=Dict(:Na => 1.0, :Cl => -1.0))
all_ions = Group(:all, AllSelection())

sim = Simulation(system;
    groups=Groups(all_ions),
    integrator=Integrator(
        dt=0.1,                                   # in √(amu Å²/eV) ≈ 10.18 fs units
        forces=[
            LennardJones(epsilon=0.01, sigma=2.8, cutoff=9.0, pairs=:neighborlist,
                         neighborlist=CellList(capacity=512)),
            PME(coulomb_prefactor=14.399645,      # eV·Å/e²
                real_cutoff=9.0, real_space_tolerance=1e-5,
                mesh_spacing=1.0, order=5),
        ],
        methods=[ConstantVolume(all_ions)],
    ),
    observables=[ThermodynamicObservable(all_ions)],
    precision=Float32,
)
run!(sim, Stage(:nve, steps=1000; progress=false))

Replace the PME(...) entry by PPPM(...; differentiation=:ik) or ESP(coulomb_prefactor=14.399645, real_cutoff=9.0, tolerance=1e-5) to switch methods. The short-range vdW force keeps its own cutoff; the shared neighbor list covers the larger of the two cutoffs.

Units and conventions

The engine has no built-in unit system. The pair energy is

\[U_{ij} = C\,\frac{q_i q_j}{r_{ij}},\qquad C = \frac{\texttt{coulomb\_prefactor}}{\varepsilon_r},\]

and C enters every term (real space, reciprocal space, self energy, exceptions, background, virial) exactly once.

QuantityUnit
positions, box, cutoffs, mesh_spacingL
alpha (Gaussian split)1/L
chargescaller's Q
coulomb_prefactorE·L/Q²
energy, virialE
forcesE/L

Common choices: ≈138.935458 for nm, kJ/mol and elementary charges; ≈14.399645 for Å, eV and elementary charges; 1 for reduced units. Thermostat inputs stay kT in energy units. ESP shape parameters (split_shape, window_shape) are dimensionless and distinct from alpha.

Ewald split

For a split kernel χ (dimensionless, ∫χ = 1) with a(t) = ∫₀ᵗ χ, t = r/r_c:

\[\frac{1}{r} = \frac{1-a(t)}{r} + \frac{a(t)}{r},\qquad \hat g(k) = \frac{4\pi}{k^2}\int \chi(x)\cos(k r_c x)\,dx .\]

The Gaussian choice gives erfc(αr)/r, ĝ = 4π e^{-k²/4α²}/k² and the self energy −Cα/√π Σqᵢ². The ESP choice uses the order-zero prolate spheroidal wave function ψ₀ᶜ on [0, 1], so the real-space part vanishes exactly at r_c. Its self energy is −(C/2) χ(0)/r_c Σqᵢ².

Mesh

Charges are assigned per node with the window W (B-spline of order P, or the prolate window ψ₀ᶜ¹(2x/P)), transformed with an unnormalised forward FFT, multiplied by the influence function G (which contains C/V) and transformed back with an unnormalised inverse. The mesh energy is ½ Σ G|b̂|²; analytic-differentiation forces are the exact gradient of this discrete energy. Influence functions:

  • SPME: G = (C/V) ĝ(k) Π_d D_d, with SPME's exact discrete B-spline factor D;
  • P3M-AD/ik: Hockney–Eastwood optimal alias sums for the selected derivative;
  • ESP: by default the optimal AD influence function with alias sums of the prolate window transform (influence=:optimal). With influence=:deconvolution it is the plain ĝ/φ̂² of the paper's eq. (9).

Energies, virials and observables

  • Per-particle energies. Real-space and exception pair energies are split ½/½. The mesh contributes ½ qᵢ uᵢ (uᵢ = interpolated mesh potential), which sums exactly to the mesh energy. The self energy is local, and a neutralising background is distributed in proportion to qᵢ Q. System totals (sum(st.Epot), thermodynamic observables, NHC/CSVR extended Hamiltonians) therefore include electrostatics automatically. Subgroup energies follow this documented convention.
  • Virial. Real-space and exception virials are per-particle (in virial_nonbonded). The reciprocal-space tensor is the strain derivative of the discrete mesh energy at fixed fractional coordinates. It has no per-particle decomposition and is stored globally in st.virial_global. virial_tensor(st) and full-system observables include it; virial_tensor(st; part=:reciprocal) returns it alone. Group observables that request a virial for a strict subgroup raise an error while electrostatics is attached. Per-particle GSD virial output contains the local terms only.
  • Diagnostics. electrostatic_energy returns the real, self, reciprocal and background components plus the reciprocal virial at the current coordinates.

Energies and virials are refreshed on force evaluations with compute_energy=true, like every other term in the engine.

Exceptions

Scaled or excluded pairs (e.g. 1–2/1–3 exclusions, 1–4 scaling) are handled by subtracting (1 − s) C qᵢqⱼ/r at the pair's minimum-image distance, independently of the cutoff. Periodic images keep the full interaction. The real-space sum itself never skips pairs, and Lennard-Jones exclusions never delete Coulomb pairs. Workflow descriptors collect exceptions from exclusions=:topology (Topology.exclusions, scale 0), exclude_bonded=true, and scaled_pairs=[(i, j, s), …].

Charged systems

net_charge=:error (default) rejects a nonzero total charge. With net_charge=:background a uniform neutralising background adds −C π Q²/(2α²V) (Gaussian) or −C π r_c² m₂ Q²/V (prolate). It contributes the same amount to each diagonal virial component and adds no force.

Lifecycle, reordering and restarts

  • The solver holds no particle-array pointers; positions, charges and force slots are passed on every call. Force-slot swaps, Brownian midpoint evaluations and spatial reordering are therefore safe. Charges are permuted with their particles. Reordering is suspended while exception rows are attached.
  • set_charges! replaces charges and re-checks the net-charge policy. detach_electrostatics! removes the solver.
  • GSD frames written while a solver is attached store particles/charge and log/electrostatics/settings (method, prefactor, εᵣ, cutoff, α or shapes, order, mesh, alias order, net-charge policy, precision), plus log/electrostatics/exceptions. ParticleSystem.from_gsd(path) restores the charges, and electrostatics_from_gsd returns the exact descriptor. FFT plans and workspaces are rebuilt on load.

Choosing parameters

Parameters are explicit; nothing is tuned behind the user's back.

  • PME/PPPM. Choose real_cutoff, then alpha, or real_space_tolerance (erfc(α r_c) = tol, a real-space potential-tail criterion rather than a force error). Then refine mesh/mesh_spacing and order until the measured force error meets the target.

  • ESP. tolerance = ε sets the split shape (ψ₀ᶜ(1) = ε for the L²-normalised prolate), the window shape (from ε/2), the window width (Table 2 of Liang et al. 2026: 5, 5, 6, 7, 8 for ε = 1e-3 … 1e-5) and mesh_spacing = π r_c / c. The measured force error is typically 1–2 ε and does not decrease further when only the mesh is refined: the prolate window's alias level is fixed in grid units. To reach a target Δ, tighten ε (e.g. ε ≈ Δ/2) instead of the mesh.

  • Measured targeting. tune_electrostatics(st, force; target) finds the cheapest mesh (PME/PPPM) or tolerance (ESP) whose measured error against a high-accuracy ESP reference at the same cutoff meets target. It returns an explicit descriptor, so nothing is retuned silently on restart.

  • Fastest setting. tune_electrostatics(st, force; target, objective=:time) searches more knobs at the same real-space cutoff:

    • for PME/PPPM, the order (4–6 / 5–7), the Ewald α (real-space tail target × 0.01 … 0.3) and the mesh;
    • for ESP, ε and the window width.

    It measures each candidate through the state's own force evaluation (vdW term and nonbonded backend included) and returns the fastest one that meets target, with the full candidate table.

    The Float32 reference is an order-6 PME on a fine grid. Float32 ESP references at very tight ε are not accurate enough, because wide prolate windows amplify single-precision rounding.

Low-level API

st = build_simulation(N=N, box=(L, L, L), nonbonded=:none, cutoff=rc,
                      precision=:f64, charges=q)
copyto!(st.rx, x); copyto!(st.ry, y); copyto!(st.rz, z)
attach_electrostatics!(st, PME(coulomb_prefactor=1.0, real_cutoff=rc, alpha=1.0,
                               mesh=(32, 32, 32)); exceptions=[(1, 2, 0.0)])
SimulationCore.evaluate_forces_into_f!(st, true)
electrostatic_energy(st)

build_simulation(...; neighbor_cutoff=rc) widens the neighbor list of a vdW state to the Coulomb cutoff; attach_electrostatics! also widens a dense list automatically.

ParticleDynamics.Workflow.PME — Type
PME(; coulomb_prefactor, real_cutoff, alpha=nothing, real_space_tolerance=nothing,
      mesh=nothing, mesh_spacing=nothing, order=4, relative_permittivity=1.0,
      net_charge=:error, boundary=:tin_foil, real_space_modifier=:none,
      exclusions=:topology, exclude_bonded=false, scaled_pairs=(),
      pairs=:neighborlist, neighborlist=nothing)

Smooth particle-mesh Ewald (Essmann et al. 1995) with analytic differentiation, computed entirely on the GPU.

Units are the caller's: U = C qᵢqⱼ/r with C = coulomb_prefactor / relative_permittivity, so coulomb_prefactor carries E·L/Q² (≈138.935458 for nm/kJ mol⁻¹/e, ≈14.399645 for Å/eV/e, or 1 in reduced units). alpha is in inverse length units; alternatively real_space_tolerance sets it from erfc(α r_c) = tol (a real-space potential tail criterion, not a force error). mesh=(K₁,K₂,K₃) or mesh_spacing (FFT friendly sizes are chosen) and the B-spline order (stencil width) set the reciprocal accuracy.

Exceptions: exclusions=:topology excludes Topology.exclusions pairs (scale 0); exclude_bonded=true also excludes bonded pairs; scaled_pairs lists (i, j, scale) triples (e.g. 1–4 scaling) and overrides the other two. Each exception removes or scales the pair's minimum-image interaction while its periodic images remain.

net_charge=:error rejects charged systems; :background adds a uniform neutralising background (energy and virial, no force).

real_space_modifier=:potential_shift subtracts the short-range pair energy at the cutoff from every non-excluded pair inside it (energy only; forces are unchanged), matching the GROMACS coulomb-modifier = Potential-shift Hamiltonian; the default :none is the unshifted Ewald sum.

ParticleDynamics.Workflow.PPPM — Type
PPPM(; coulomb_prefactor, real_cutoff, alpha=nothing, real_space_tolerance=nothing,
       mesh=nothing, mesh_spacing=nothing, order=5, differentiation=:ad,
       alias_order=2, kwargs...)

Particle–particle particle–mesh (P3M) with the Hockney–Eastwood optimal influence function for B-spline assignment. differentiation=:ad uses analytic gradients of the assignment weights (one forward and one inverse transform, influence function optimised for AD); :ik forms the electric field in reciprocal space (one forward and three inverse transforms). Alias sums run over |n| ≤ alias_order images per dimension. All other keywords match PME.

The ik forces are not the exact gradient of the reported mesh energy; the energy and virial are those of the discrete influence function.

ParticleDynamics.Workflow.ESP — Type
ESP(; coulomb_prefactor, real_cutoff, tolerance=1e-4, split_shape=nothing,
      window_shape=nothing, order=nothing, mesh=nothing, mesh_spacing=nothing,
      kwargs...)

Ewald summation with prolates (Liang, Lu, Barnett, Greengard & Jiang 2026): the Gaussian split is replaced by one built from the prolate spheroidal wave function ψ₀ᶜ, and B-spline assignment by a prolate window of width order grid points. The real-space part vanishes exactly at real_cutoff.

Defaults follow the paper's selection rules for a target relative force error tolerance ε: split_shape c with ψ₀ᶜ(1) = ε (L²-normalised), window_shape from ε/2, order from Table 2 of the paper (5, 5, 6, 7, 8 for ε = 1e-3, 5e-4, 1e-4, 5e-5, 1e-5), and mesh_spacing = π r_c / c. All dimensionless shapes are distinct from the Gaussian alpha (inverse length). Treat the defaults as starting points and measure the error for production settings.

influence=:optimal (default) uses the analytic-differentiation optimal influence function with alias sums over |n| ≤ alias_order images of the prolate window transform, which compensates the aliasing of the truncated window. influence=:deconvolution uses the plain window deconvolution ĝ/φ̂² of the paper's equation (9). The remaining keywords match PME (alpha/ real_space_tolerance do not apply).

ParticleDynamics.SimulationCore.attach_electrostatics! — Function
attach_electrostatics!(st, spec::Electrostatics.ElectrostaticsSpec;
                       charges=nothing, exceptions=()) -> st

Attach a long-range Coulomb solver to a 3D state. charges (build-time particle order, caller's charge unit) replace st.charge; when omitted the state must already carry charges. exceptions lists (i, j, scale) triples in build-time ids: the pair's home-image interaction C qᵢqⱼ/r is scaled by scale (0 excludes it) while periodic images keep the full interaction.

Setup validates the box (real_cutoff below half the shortest box length, orthorhombic 3D), plans cuFFT transforms and computes the influence function. The state moves to the cluster-pair backend (charged systems), which keeps its storage order unsorted.

attach_electrostatics!(st, force::ElectrostaticsForce; charges=nothing, exceptions=())

Low-level convenience: attach the solver described by a workflow descriptor to an existing state.

ParticleDynamics.SimulationCore.electrostatic_energy — Function
electrostatic_energy(st) -> NamedTuple

Diagnostic breakdown of the electrostatic energy at the current coordinates: real (real-space pairs plus exception corrections), self, reciprocal (mesh), background and total, plus the reciprocal virial tensor reciprocal_virial (xx, yy, zz, xy, xz, yz). Uses scratch buffers; the state's force and energy buffers are untouched. Not for the hot path.

ParticleDynamics.Workflow.tune_electrostatics — Function
tune_electrostatics(st, force; target, reference_tolerance=target/1000,
                    max_mesh=512, spacings=nothing) -> NamedTuple

Choose the cheapest accuracy setting of force that meets a measured relative RMS force error target on the charges and coordinates of st (a state whose neighbor list covers force.real_cutoff).

With objective=:time the search is over every knob that keeps the real-space cutoff, and the fastest complete force evaluation of st (its own vdW term and nonbonded backend included) that meets target wins:

  • PME and PPPM: assignment order (orders, default 4–6 for PME and 5–7 for PPPM), the Ewald α through the real-space tail (real_space_tolerances, default target × (0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3)) and the coarsest passing mesh spacing for each pair;
  • ESP: the tolerance ε and the window width (default width and one point narrower or wider, within the shape's bound).

The error is measured on the Coulomb forces through the state's force path (forces without electrostatics are subtracted), against an ESP reference at reference_tolerance (Float64 states) or an order-6 PME on a grid of spacing rc/14 (Float32 states, ~5e-6 accurate). The state's solver is restored afterwards. Returns `(force, error, timems, mesh, candidates), wherecandidates` lists every passing setting with its error and time.

The reference is an ESP solve at the same cutoff with prolate tolerance reference_tolerance; ESP converges at fixed cutoff, so it reuses the state's neighbor list. For PME and PPPM the mesh spacing is refined (cutoff, alpha/real_space_tolerance and order are kept). For ESP the tolerance ε is tightened with the paper's rules for shape, width and spacing; refining an ESP mesh alone saturates near ε.

Returns (force, error, time_ms, mesh, reference_error_bound). force is an explicit descriptor (fixed mesh, no hidden retuning), error the measured error against the reference, time_ms the median force-call time and reference_error_bound the reference's own measured change when ε is halved. Electrostatic exceptions are not included in the comparison (they are identical for every candidate).

ParticleDynamics.Electrostatics.ElectrostaticsSpec — Type
ElectrostaticsSpec

Validated, host-side description of a long-range Coulomb solver. Built by the workflow descriptors PME, PPPM and ESP; only the combinations those constructors produce are supported:

influencesplitwindowdifferentiation
:spmeGaussianB-splineanalytic
:p3m_adGaussianB-splineanalytic, optimal AD influence
:p3m_ikGaussianB-splineik (three field transforms)
:espprolateprolateanalytic, optimal AD influence (alias sums)
:esp_deconvolutionprolateprolateanalytic, plain window deconvolution

Fields: coulomb_prefactor, relative_permittivity (C = prefactor/εᵣ), real_cutoff, split, window, mesh ((K₁,K₂,K₃)) or mesh_spacing, influence, alias_order (P3M alias sums), net_charge (:error or :background), boundary (:tin_foil), and real_space_modifier: :none (unshifted), or :potential_shift, which subtracts the short-range pair energy at the cutoff from every non-excluded pair inside it (energy only, forces unchanged; the GROMACS Potential-shift convention). Provenance

The ESP implementation follows the mathematics published by Liang, Lu, Barnett, Greengard and Jiang, Accelerating molecular dynamics simulations using fast Ewald summation with prolates, Nat. Commun. 17, 6681 (2026). The prolate functions are computed from the Legendre expansion of the prolate differential operator. The split and window shapes follow the paper's rule ψ₀ᶜ(1) = ε, and Table 2 is reproduced as a test. No source code or coefficient tables from the published reference implementations were used. References: Essmann et al., J. Chem. Phys. 103, 8577 (1995) (SPME); Hockney and Eastwood (1988) and Deserno and Holm, J. Chem. Phys. 109, 7678 (1998) (P3M); Ballenegger, Cerdà and Holm, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 8, 936 (2012) (influence functions for AD/ik).
